  FIRST PRINCIPLES:
  - The bed datum and the carved surface are MUTUALLY dependent, not
  sequentially dependent.
    bed = f(terrain)  [assign samples terrain]
    terrain = g(bed)  [carve cuts toward the bed profile]
    influence_radius = h(terrain − bed)  [HydrologyTuning.influence(width, Δ)]
    Any single-pass ordering is wrong by construction. The only correct answers
  are (a) iterate to a
    fixed point, or (b) break the cycle by making one side independent of the
  other.
  - Both operators are monotone non-increasing and bounded below. The carve is
  cut-only
    (h = min(elevs[i], lut), and lut is floored at the primitive's own bed
  elevation); assign clamps
    beds down toward sampled terrain and floors them at the terminal DRAIN
  elevation (0 at ocean).
    A descending sequence bounded below converges. This is the single most
  important structural fact:
    ANY iteration scheme here terminates, and it is why iteration is a
  legitimate answer at all.
  - Determinism is non-negotiable. Tiles are memoized and rebuilt on demand;
  chunk gen is
    multithreaded; a world must regenerate identically. Any iteration must stop
  on a fixed count or a
    deterministic float predicate, and any per-unit sweep must have a canonical,
  total ordering.
  - Order-independence is worth paying for. The prior deepthink on this system
  (memory:
    river-carve-first-redesign) explicitly chose min-compositing because "min is
  order-independent →
    no confluence double-deepen." The shipped code instead uses an
  order-dependent smooth-min
    recurrence sorted by DESCENDING influence, which is what makes incremental
  carving hazardous.
  - Tileability: 512² tile with PAD=1 halo, influence up to 64 px. The
  interaction radius already
    exceeds the halo (README documents local-shell tile seams). No scheme may
  widen that coupling.
  - Cost is on the world-gen path. carveRiverInfluence is O(Σ primitives ×
  footprint area); a
    64-influence primitive stamps 128×128 = 16k lattice points. Multiplying full
  carves by the number
    of source→drain walks is not affordable; multiplying by a small constant is.

  KEY CONCEPTS:
  - Influence carve (`carveRiverInfluence`, tile-level, 514² lattice): broad
  valley/floodplain shell.
    The thing under discussion.
  - Bed carve (`computeRiverGrid` from
  `PopulateNoiseStep.fineGrainedPrimitivePass`, per chunk, 16²):
    cuts the trench, cut-only against the already-carved shell as ambient. Out
  of scope per the user.
  - Walk: a maximal SOURCE→DRAIN path. NOT a partition of the graph — the
  network is an in-forest, so
    sibling walks SHARE their downstream trunk. Carving "one walk" recarves
  shared trunk primitives.
    This is the first structural problem with the proposed unit of work.
  - Cascade direction: assign's dependency is genuinely BIDIRECTIONAL along a
  path. Junction elevation
    = min over incoming (upstream→downstream), but every bed is floored at its
  terminal drain's
    elevation via `resolveDrainElevations` (downstream→upstream). "Changes
  cascade downstream" is only
    half the dependency.
  - Spatial coupling ≠ topological coupling. Channel A's 64 px footprint lowers
  terrain under channel
    B even when A and B share no ancestry. A per-walk sweep therefore does not
  decompose the problem;
    it only reorders it.
  - Merge law / comparator: `HydrologicalPrimitive.comparator` sorts
  RiverPrimitives by DESCENDING
    `influence()`. The carve is a sequential recurrence on `dist[]` in that
  order. Feeding primitives
    in per-walk batches violates the global sort and changes output. Verified at
    `features/HydrologicalPrimitive.java:41-49` and `profile/README.md` "Merge
  law".
  - Ambient re-read: `carvePrimitiveInfluence` reads `elevs[i]` per primitive
  (`h = min(elevs[i], …)`)
    while `acc` is separately seeded from the same buffer and applied only at
  the end. So within one
    call, ambient is frozen; across calls it is not. This is what makes "carve
  into the buffer, then
    re-read it" a semantically different operator from one big carve.

  flow? by Strahler?

● DIRECT ANALOGIES:

  - GIS stream burning (AGREE, Hellweger 1997; ANUDEM, Hutchinson 1989): the
  exact artifact. "Burned
    channel floats above the DEM" is the canonical failure of post-hoc network
  burning. The two mature
    answers diverge instructively. AGREE burns against a *smoothed* DEM and
  applies a wide buffer plus
    a narrow trench — i.e. it derives the burn depth from a conditioned surface,
  not the raw one.
    ANUDEM refuses the post-hoc step entirely and enforces drainage as a
  CONSTRAINT during
    interpolation — datum and surface solved together. Nobody in that literature
  solves it by
    sequencing a burn more carefully. LESSON: the two known-good shapes are
  "condition the datum" and
    "solve jointly"; "burn in a better order" is not on the list.

  - Priority-flood depression filling with epsilon gradient (Barnes, Lehman &
  Mulla 2014): a monotone
    descending constraint over a graph, resolved in ONE priority-ordered sweep
  instead of iterating.
    `ChannelElevationAssigner.assign` is already this — topological order via
  `pendingIncoming`
    counters, floored by `resolveDrainElevations`. LESSON: the assign side is
  already the right
    algorithm; it is not what needs restructuring. The coupling to the carve is.

  - This project's own prior design round (memory `river-carve-first-redesign`,
  2026-07-13): a
    deepthink on this same carve recommended "per-pixel absolute valley-floor
  target, **min-composited**
    into the buffer (min is order-independent → no confluence double-deepen)".
  The shipped code
    instead uses the order-dependent sequential smooth-min `dist[]` recurrence
  sorted by descending
    influence. LESSON: the order-free choice was already reasoned for once and
  lost in implementation.
    The current symptom is partly the predicted consequence of that loss. Strong
  prior — this is the
    same system telling us the same thing twice.

  - This project's own `GlobalNetworkBuilder` step 1: it carves globally BEFORE
  running
    `Drainage.fillSinks`/`computeDrainageDirection`, explicitly so the derived
  field sees valleys
    instead of raw decoder noise, and the README marks the ordering
  load-bearing. LESSON: "carve first
    so the next derived quantity sees the cut" is already this codebase's idiom,
  and the user's
    proposal is that idiom at finer granularity. The instinct has precedent; the
  granularity is what
    is in question.

  - GPU hydraulic-erosion simulators (Mei/Decaudin/Hu 2007; Šťava 2008): a
  coupled height/water system
    with mutual feedback, iterated to *approximate* steady state with a FIXED
  step count plus damping,
    never to true convergence, because the visual result stabilizes long before
  the numerics do.
    LESSON: fixed iteration count + damping is the industry-normal answer to
  exactly this coupling
    shape. Converging "until beds stop moving" is over-engineering; converging
  for N=3 is normal.

  CROSS-DOMAIN ANALOGIES:

  - Numerical linear algebra: Jacobi vs Gauss–Seidel vs SOR. GS converges ~2×
  faster per sweep but is
    inherently sequential and makes the ANSWER depend on sweep order; Jacobi is
  order-free and
    parallel. The standard compromise when order-freedom matters is red-black
  ordering, or just
    accepting Jacobi's rate. INSIGHT: the user's proposal is Gauss–Seidel. Their
  own answer to the
    merge-law question ("order-free is better") is the Jacobi preference. These
  are in tension, and
    the tension resolves toward Jacobi here because we need ~3 rounds of visual
  agreement, not
    convergence to 1e-12 — GS's rate advantage buys nothing we need, and costs
  the one property we
    said we want.

  - Position-based dynamics / constraint relaxation (Müller 2007; Macklin's
  XPBD): same GS-vs-Jacobi
    fork, and the field settled on Jacobi + few iterations + stiffness damping,
  chosen for determinism
    and parallelism over convergence rate. INSIGHT: 3 Jacobi rounds beat 1 GS
  sweep on every criterion
    in step 3 except raw convergence rate, which is the criterion we care least
  about.

  - Implicit-surface / SDF composition: `min` is associative and commutative,
  hence order-free.
    Polynomial `smin` is commutative but NOT associative, which is why the
  current sequential `dist[]`
    recurrence is order-dependent. But the exponential form, `-log(Σ
  exp(-k·hᵢ))/k`, and its cheap
    cousin, the **normalized weighted average** `Σwᵢhᵢ / Σwᵢ` with `wᵢ` a
  footprint falloff, ARE
    order-free AND smooth. INSIGHT — the most valuable one here: the user does
  not have to trade
    blend quality for order-freedom. Replacing the sequential `dist[i] =
  (1-w)·dist[i] + w·d`
    recurrence with an accumulated `(Σwh, Σw)` pair makes the carve commutative
  over primitives
    without flattening the blend to a hard min. That single change is what makes
  every other option
    on the table safe, and it is confined to `HydrologyProfileInprinter`.

  - Dataflow analysis / worklist algorithms over monotone frameworks: for
  monotone transfer functions
    on a lattice with finite descending chains, the worklist reaches the SAME
  fixed point regardless
    of pop order — order affects only time-to-converge. INSIGHT, and it is a
  double-edged one: the
    user's "re-assign only where elevation changed, cascading downstream" IS a
  worklist algorithm, and
    the theory says its order does not matter — **but only if you iterate to a
  true fixed point.**
    Under a hard iteration cap (which criterion 2 requires), you stop mid-chain
  and order becomes part
    of the output again. So the theoretical order-independence of the worklist
  does not survive the
    cap that determinism-under-a-budget forces on us.

  - Self-adjusting / incremental computation (Acar 2005): change propagation
  pays off only when the
    dependency graph is sparse; dense graphs make the bookkeeping cost exceed
  the recompute cost.
    INSIGHT: here a single primitive's 128×128 footprint covers ~6% of a 514²
  tile, and a channel
    carries hundreds of them, so one channel's dirty set is a large fraction of
  the tile. The
    "only where it changed" optimization probably has a dirty set of ≈
  everything. Incrementality is
    unlikely to pay for its complexity.

  ANTI-PATTERNS:

  - **Fixing a two-artifact consistency bug with a convergence loop.** The
  relief is carved from
    `assign` #2 (`LocalNetworkBuilder.java:44`) and the R-tree publishes
  `assign` #3 (`:62`, collected
    at `RiverProvider.java:142`). You can converge perfectly and still publish a
  different iterate than
    you carved. No iteration scheme repairs this; it must be fixed as its own
  defect, first, and it
    may account for a large share of the visible symptom.

  - **Gauss–Seidel with an arbitrary sweep order in a system that must be
  deterministic and tileable.**
    The order becomes part of the world's identity. Worse than aesthetic: a
  river crossing a tile
    boundary is traced into two different graphs with two different walk sets,
  so the two tiles sweep
    the shared reach in different orders and disagree at the seam — on top of
  the PAD=1 seam the
    README already documents.

  - **Iterating a loop with an `abs` in the feedback term and no damping.**
  `influence(width, Δ)` uses
    `Δ = |terrain − bed|`. The absolute value is a trap: the loop is
  self-damping while terrain is
    above bed (carving shrinks Δ, shrinks influence), but once terrain is cut
  BELOW a bed, Δ starts
    growing again and the influence radius *expands* toward the 64 cap.
  Re-collecting primitives
    between rounds without re-assigning first is exactly the ordering that
  triggers it. Any iteration

  LENS 2 — NUMERICAL ANALYST

  Formally: bₙ = A(Eₙ), Eₙ₊₁ = K(Eₙ, C(bₙ, E₀)). K is cut-only so Eₙ₊₁ ≤ Eₙ; A
  is monotone in E; both descend, and E is bounded below by the bed, which is
  bounded below by 0. Monotone + bounded ⇒ convergence is guaranteed, and in
  float the descending chain is finite so it terminates.

  Convergence rate: along a centreline the carve pulls E toward bed with the
  final blend weight w = 1 − clamp(dist,0,1), which reaches ≈0.7 for narrow and
  ≈1.0 for wide channels. Bed error contracts geometrically at rate (1−w). 2–3 
  rounds gets within a few percent — your 2–4× budget is not a guess, it is the
  right number.

  Two things I nearly got wrong, both material:

  carve∘carve ≠ carve. carveRiverInfluence re-seeds acc from the buffer it is
  about to overwrite, so carving twice with identical primitives pulls further
  toward the target than carving once. Under naive iteration, the round count 
  becomes a tuning knob that deepens valleys even when beds have stopped moving.
  The fix is clean and I'd make it non-negotiable: carve a fresh clone of the 
  pristine base[0] every round. Then Eₙ = K(E₀, C(A(Eₙ₋₁), E₀)), only the beds
  iterate, nothing compounds, and the final artifact is "one carve, with
  converged beds" — the same kind of object the pipeline produces today. Iterate
  the beds, not the terrain.

  The abs in influence(width, Δ) is safe only under the current intra-round 
  order. Δ = |E − b| self-damps while terrain sits above bed and self-amplifies
  once it drops below. Since the round runs assign → collect → carve, Δ is
  always measured after the bed has been corrected, so the amplifying branch is
  never entered. Keep that order; do not hoist collect out of the loop.

  Verdict on (B), your per-walk Gauss–Seidel: admissible but strictly dominated.
  Walks do not partition an in-forest — sibling walks share their downstream
  trunk, so trunk primitives get carved once per upstream source, and with
  carve∘carve ≠ carve the trunk over-deepens relative to its headwaters. It
  reintroduces order-dependence (a cap forbids the worklist theorem's
  order-independence), and a river crossing a tile boundary is enumerated into
  different walk sets on each side → a new seam. Its only advantage, faster
  per-sweep convergence, buys nothing when 3 Jacobi rounds already suffice. (D) 
  predict/correct collapses into candidate C on inspection — predicting the
  post-carve surface requires a bed-independent proxy, which is candidate C.

  LENS 3 — MERGE-LAW ENGINEER

  The commutative replacement: per primitive compute wᵢ = falloff(dᵢ) and target
  hᵢ, both pure functions of (point, primitive). Accumulate Σwh, Σw, and W = 1

  and max are all order-free, so double-deepen becomes structurally impossible.
  typeMask's w > 0.5 selector becomes an argmax track.

  2. WHY WHOLE-TILE ROUNDS BEAT PER-WALK SWEEPS

  Your proposal is Gauss–Seidel; the alternative is Jacobi. GS converges faster
  per sweep, which is the
  one property that buys nothing here — the contraction rate along a centreline
  is `(1 − w)` with
  `w ≈ 0.7–1.0`, so **2–3 whole-tile rounds already converge**, matching the
  budget you approved. Against
  that, per-walk carries three costs the whole-tile form does not:
    - Walks do not partition an in-forest. Sibling walks share their downstream
  trunk, so trunk
      primitives get carved once per upstream source, and (see §3) the carve
  compounds — trunks
      over-deepen relative to headwaters.
    - Sweep order becomes part of the world's identity, and a river crossing a
  tile boundary is
      enumerated into different walk sets on each side → a new seam on top of
  the `PAD = 1` one.
    - Per-walk batches break the globally sorted merge recurrence.
  The worklist theorem that would rescue order-independence only holds at a
  *true* fixed point; a hard
  iteration cap — which determinism-under-a-budget requires — puts the order
  back into the output.

  3. THE NON-OBVIOUS PART: ITERATE THE BEDS, NOT THE TERRAIN

  `carveRiverInfluence` re-seeds its accumulator from the buffer it is about to
  overwrite, so
  **`carve∘carve ≠ carve`** — carving twice with identical primitives pulls
  further toward the target
  than carving once. Under a naive loop, the round count silently becomes a
  valley-depth knob, and the
  terrain keeps descending even after the beds have stopped moving.

  The fix makes the whole scheme clean: **carve a fresh clone of the pristine
  `base[0]` every round.**
  Only the beds iterate; nothing compounds; the final artifact is "one carve,
  with converged beds" —
  the same *kind* of object the pipeline produces today, not a new accumulated
  one.

  What is actually wrong

  The influence profile outside the floodplain is bed + max(0, perp − 
  floodPlainLength) (RosgenProfile.delta → valleyDelta), a 45° valley wall
  clipped against terrain by min. So a channel with bed 100 cuts a point 30 px
  away down to ≈128. A neighbouring channel whose bed was assigned at 130
  against the uncarved surface is now floating 2 above the ground. That is "the
  influences are really large" made quantitative.

  But the code already tries to patch this, and the patch is the other half of
  what you're seeing. LocalNetworkBuilder runs assign #2 → carve → re-seed →
  assign #3, and publishes #3 while the relief was carved toward #2. The carve
  is cut-only, so every input to assign weakly decreases and B3 ≤ B2 pointwise.
  That gives two horns:

  - publish B2 (delete assign #3) → beds float where a neighbour cut through
    them;
  - publish B3 (today) → beds track the surface, but the shell was built around
    B2, so floodplains terrace and trenches over-incise.

  You can't fix one without restoring the other. The pipeline is a fixed-point 
  iteration stopped at an odd half-step — which is exactly why you see floating
  in some reaches and buried in others. I went in expecting to find that a cheap
  publish-boundary fix would explain most of it; it doesn't, and that surprised

  Why not per-walk

  Your proposal is Gauss–Seidel; whole-tile rounds are Jacobi. GS converges
  faster per sweep — the one property that buys nothing here, because the
  contraction rate along a centreline is (1 − w) with w ≈ 0.7–1.0, so 2–3 
  whole-tile rounds converge. Against that, per-walk costs you three things:

  - Walks don't partition an in-forest. Sibling walks share their downstream
    trunk, so trunk primitives get carved once per upstream source — and the
    carve compounds (below), so trunks over-deepen relative to headwaters.
  - Sweep order becomes world identity, and a river crossing a tile boundary is
    enumerated into different walk sets on each side → a new seam on top of the
    PAD = 1 one.
  - Per-walk batches break the globally sorted merge recurrence.

  The worklist theorem that would rescue order-independence holds only at a true
  fixed point; a hard iteration cap — which determinism under a budget requires
  — puts the order back into the output.

  The part that isn't obvious

  carveRiverInfluence re-seeds its accumulator from the buffer it's about to
  overwrite, so carve∘carve ≠ carve. Under a naive loop the round count silently
  becomes a valley-depth knob, and terrain keeps descending after the beds have
  stopped.

  The fix makes everything else fall into place: carve a fresh clone of the 
  pristine base[0] every round. Only the beds iterate, nothing compounds, and
  the final artifact is "one carve with converged beds" — the same kind of
  object the pipeline makes today.

  And the fixed point is worth checking, because it's the real argument that
  this is correct rather than merely stable. At the centreline the LUT is
  clamped to ≥ elevation, so bed = (1−W)E₀ + W·bed ⟹ bed = E₀: at convergence
  the channel lies on the ground and the carve does nothing at the centreline —
  except where assign's monotone descent or the drain floor binds, which is
  exactly where a channel must cut through a rise. The flanks are untouched by
  this; the V-valley still cuts wherever E₀(p) > E₀(centre) + p − fpl.

  So expect valleys to read shallower and tighter along reaches that already 
  descend, with the cut concentrating at rises. That's the correct behaviour,
  and it's your sharpest falsification test: if a converged reach still shows a
  deep centreline cut, the loop isn't converging. If you prefer today's deeper
  valleys, the honest lever is floodPlainLength or the bed target — not stale
  beds.

● You're right, and this breaks the specific claim I made earlier. I checked
  that a channel's own Δ self-damps under carving and concluded the intra-round
  ordering made the abs safe. That was only half the picture: the neighbour path
  doesn't damp, it grows monotonically, and your scenario is the case that
  exposes it.

  The engine, named precisely

  The divergence comes from one coupling: influence is recomputed each round as 
  a function of |pristine − bed|, while the carve drives bed monotonically 
  downward. Pristine is fixed and beds only descend, so Δ only grows, so
  influence only grows. There is no term anywhere that can shrink it.

  Your trunk/tributary case, with illustrative numbers (mountain at 180, trunk
  bed 60, tributary point 50 px from the trunk, influence_W = 64, fpl_W = 20,
  influence_T = 2):

  - W's contribution: d = 50/64 = 0.78, threshold 0.31, so dd = 0.68; mask = 1 −
    1/(0.1·64+1) = 0.865. Target h = min(180, 60 + 30) = 90.
  - T's contribution: dd = 0, mask = 1 − 1/(0.1·2+1) = 0.167. Target h = 180.

  Sequential merge (W first, descending influence): dist: 1 → 0.723 → 0.602;
  acc: 180 → 102.2 → 115.2; final weight 0.398; E = 154.

  This is exactly the perverse bit you described: T raises the application 
  weight (dist drops to 0.602 because T is right there) while contributing only
  16.7% of its own height. It strengthens the application of W's low value.
  Without T the point would sit at 158; T's presence pushes it to 154 — and T's
  bed was 180.

  Next round: Δ_T = |180 − 154| = 26 → influence_T = 2·√27·w ≈ 10·w. mask_T
  jumps 0.167 → 0.51, and T's footprint goes from 2 px to ~10 px, touching
  neighbours it never touched. Those drop, their Δ grows, their footprints grow.
  Each round the affected set can expand by up to MAX_INFLUENCE_RADIUS. Bounded
  only by the 64 cap and the round count — which is precisely the N-dependence
  I said we had to avoid.

  And your two escape routes are correctly closed. Dropping the min makes the
  carve able to raise terrain, so channels overshoot. Sampling Δ against the
  carved surface instead of pristine is the mirror failure: assign sets bed ≈ E
  on the channel, so Δ → 0, influence collapses to MIN_INFLUENCE_RADIUS, and the
  valleys vanish. Neither endpoint of that axis is usable.

  The invariant that was missing

  No footprint may depend on a quantity the carve drives monotonically. The
  carve's target (bed + max(0, p − fpl)) is allowed to move — that's the thing
  being solved for, and it contracts. The carve's support must not, because
  support growth is what percolates.

  So: compute influence once, from the round-0 assign, and hold it fixed for the
  whole loop.

  That is a one-line consequence for the thing you asked about last time —
  influence moves out of the per-round recompute and into the invariant
  template. rebuildBedAndInfluence becomes rebuildBed:

  public record RiverPointRef(
          int channelId, int ptIdx,
          double[] coord, double[] normal,
          double curvature, double width,
          RiverPrimitive.RosgenType rosgenType,
          double influence) {}          // was recomputed per round; now frozen 
  at round 0

  and the rebuild loses its surfaceElev read and its HydrologyTuning.influence
  call entirely — it reads ch.bedElev(r.ptIdx()) and nothing else. The re-sort
  also becomes unnecessary, since HydrologicalPrimitive.comparator's secondary
  key is influence, which no longer changes. Sort once, reuse the order every
  round.

  Why freezing is enough

  With footprints fixed, propagation per hop is bounded by the footprint radius
  rather than growing with it. Re-run your scenario: T's bed falls 180 → 154 →
  ~150 and stops. T's cone is 150 + p, and its support ends at p = 2, so it cuts
  essentially nothing — a tributary 20 px away is never touched. The corridor W
  eats is the 64 px it was always going to eat, because influence_W = 64 at
  round 0 already (its Δ is huge from the start). The mountain loses the 
  corridor it was designed to lose, and nothing more. The reachable set is fixed
  at round 0 and the values inside it converge monotonically, so the result is
  N-independent once converged.

  The knickpoint this leaves in T's profile — assign's bed[i] = min(bed[i-1], …)
  propagates the drop downstream but not upstream, so T's upper course stays
  high and steps down where W's cone bites — is physically a waterfall, and
  there's already a WaterfallPrimitive slot for it.

  This also revises stage 2

  I recommended the order-free Σwh/Σw, W = 1 − Π(1−wᵢ) merge. On your exact
  point it gives T = 104.6, W = 0.888, E = 113 — 40 lower than the sequential
  form's 154. The commutative form is more aggressive here, because the
  sequential recurrence's acc carries the earlier primitive's value forward at
  reduced weight while the accumulator form weights every primitive at full
  strength.

  Whether 113 or 154 is right is a genuine judgement: 113 puts T's bed on W's
  valley wall, which is arguably where a tributary descending into a big valley
  belongs. But it's a bigger visual change than I represented, and it's the same
  axis your objection is about. I'd keep stage 2 strictly after stage 1 lands
  and is judged, rather than bundling them.

  Cheap diagnostic

  Log max(influence) and mean(influence) per round. Under the frozen scheme
  they're constant by construction. If you ever try the recomputed variant,
  monotone growth across rounds is the cascade signature, and you'll see it in
  two rounds without needing to eyeball a render.

  One thing I'd still want to check before committing: whether freezing at b_0
  gives large trunks the footprint you want, since b_0 comes from assign against
  raw decoder terrain and already carries the monotone-descent and drain-floor
  pulls. If round-0 influence turns out systematically too small for trunks, the
  principled fix is to make shell influence a function of width alone
  (HydrologyTuning.influence(double) already exists and is used for margin calc)

  dependence on a round-0 estimate.

● You're right that freezing influence only stops the propagation — it doesn't
  explain why T's own bed undershot on the very first carve. That's a separate,
  structural problem in the merge, and it's why your weight-limiting experiment
  couldn't work.

  Why the mask knob can't win

  mask multiplies w, and w drives both updates: dist[i] = (1−w)·dist[i] + w·dd
  and acc[a] = (1−w)·acc[a] + w·h. So lowering T's weight lowers how much
  coverage T adds and lowers how much of T's own (high) target gets blended in.
  The two effects fight, and the result is non-monotone. Sweeping mask_T on your
  scenario, everything else held at the numbers from before (dist = 0.723, acc 
  = 102.15 after W):

  ┌─────────────┬───────┬───────────────┬───────┬───────┬───────┬─────┐
  │   mask_T    │   0   │ 0.167 (today) │  0.3  │  0.5  │  0.8  │ 1.0 │
  ├─────────────┼───────┼───────────────┼───────┼───────┼───────┼─────┤
  │ resulting E │ 158.4 │ 154.2         │ 153.1 │ 155.2 │ 166.6 │ 180 │
  └─────────────┴───────┴───────────────┴───────┴───────┴───────┴─────┘

  The curve dips before it recovers, and mask = 1 − 1/(0.1·influence + 1) puts
  small channels at 0.167 — right on the descending side. Limiting the weight
  further moved you toward 158 at best, recovering 4 units out of a 26-unit
  error. Pushing the other way "works" only at mask_T → 1, where T fully
  overrides W at its own centreline while W still wins 3 px away — a 78-high
  knife-edge ridge running along the tributary through the middle of W's valley.
  There is no setting of that knob that gives you 180 without the ridge,
  because the knob is a single scalar feeding two roles that need opposite
  treatment.

  Ordering isn't the lever either. I ran T-first through the same recurrence:
  dist: 1 → 0.833 → 0.701, acc: 180 → 180 → 102.15, E = 156.7 versus 154.2 for
  W-first. Two and a half units. The recurrence is nearly order-blind here
  because W's weight (0.865) dominates whatever it starts from.

  What's actually wrong

  dist and acc are updated with one weight, but they answer different questions
  — "how much river is at this point" and "what height do the rivers want here".
  A primitive that says "I'm here, and I want to be at 180" raises the first
  while barely moving the second, so it strengthens the application of somebody
  else's low height. A primitive lowers the terrain at its own centreline.
  That's the defect, and no reweighting fixes it because it's in the shape of
  the recurrence.

  The property you want, stated precisely: adding a primitive whose own applied 
  surface is at or above the current result must not lower the result.

  The merge that has that property

  Apply each primitive independently against ambient, then compose with min:

  E_i(p) = (1 − w_i)·E₀(p) + w_i·min(h_i(p), E₀(p))     // one primitive's own
  carved surface
  E(p)   = min over i of E_i(p)

  where w_i is a pure falloff of that primitive's own d_i — no running state, no
  dist[] array at all.

  On your scenario:

  E_W = 0.135·180 + 0.865·min(90, 180)  = 102.15
  E_T = (1−mask_T)·180 + mask_T·min(180, 180) = 180    for ANY mask_T
  E   = min(102.15, 180) = 102.15

  T's presence changes nothing, at any weight. The mask knob becomes meaningful
  again — it controls how far T's own valley extends — without contaminating
  anyone else's application.

  Other properties that fall out:
  - Order-free, since min is commutative and associative. This replaces the
    stage-2 proposal I made earlier — which I showed last turn gives 113 here,
    worse than the current 154. Withdraw that; this is the order-free form to
    use.
  - No confluence double-deepen: two coincident channels each cut to their own
    target and min picks the lower, with no compounding.
  - Cheaper: dist[] disappears entirely, and the influence path's acc triple
    collapses to a single height buffer (the water-surface slot is only used by
    the bed path). One read of E₀, one min, one write per lattice point.
  - typeMask moves from w > 0.5 on the running dist to argmin over E_i (or
    argmax over w_i) — one parallel array, same cost.

  Two things to watch. Creases where two valleys meet, since min is C⁰ —
  RosgenProfile.blendMin is already in the codebase for exactly this, and its
  docstring says why it exists rather than smoothMin: it equals min once the
  inputs differ by more than the blend range, so it softens the crease without
  sinking terrain the river cannot reach. Order-dependence survives only inside
  that narrow band. Scalloping along a channel, since consecutive primitives no
  longer accumulate coverage — bounded by the existing dx ≤ width/2 rule
  (RosgenProfile's docstring states it; collectPrimitives enforces it via dx = 
  max(ch.intakeWidth(), MIN_CONVERT_SPACING)), and second-order in practice
  because influenceLen is several times dx.

  This is still a separate fix from freezing influence

  They solve different halves and you need both. Per-primitive min fixes why T 
  undershot in the first place. Frozen influence fixes why the undershoot 
  spread. Even under the better merge, T's bed lands at 102 against pristine
  180, so Δ = 78 and a recomputed influence would still balloon.

  And a third lever, if the real complaint is the 64 px reach

  Under the new merge W still cuts to 102 at 50 px out, because its target is
  bed_W + (perp − fpl) — a 45° wall anchored on a bed 120 below the mountain
  top. If that's too aggressive, the honest knobs are valleyDelta (steepen it so
  the cone clips against terrain sooner) or a cut-depth cap relative to
  ambient, e.g. h_i = max(lut, E₀ − maxCut(width)), which bounds how far any
  single river can lower terrain no matter how deep its bed sits. That's
  independent of both changes above, and it's the direct answer to your original
  "the influences are really large" — the merge fix makes the carve correct,
  this one makes it restrained.
